# Lumpectomy-vs-Mastectomy-Understanding-Your-Breast-Cancer-Surgery-Options

### What is a lumpectomy (breast-conserving surgery)?
- Removes the tumour plus a rim of healthy tissue and keeps the rest of the breast.
- Radiation to the breast usually follows.
- Best suited to smaller tumours confined to one area, when radiation is feasible.
- Chemotherapy before surgery can sometimes shrink a larger tumour enough to allow breast conservation.

### What is a mastectomy?
- Removes the entire breast.
- Variants: total, modified radical (also removes armpit lymph nodes), and skin- or nipple-sparing.
- Generally advised for large tumours, cancer in several areas of the breast, patients who can't have radiation, or carriers of high-risk genes such as BRCA.

### Side-by-side comparison

| | Lumpectomy | Mastectomy |
|---|---|---|
| Breast | Preserved | Removed |
| Radiation | Almost always needed | Sometimes needed |
| Hospital stay | Shorter | Longer |
| Re-operation for clear margins | More likely | Less likely |
| Reconstruction | Oncoplastic reshaping possible | Implant or own-tissue reconstruction possible |
| Survival (early breast cancer) | Similar | Similar |

### What about the lymph nodes?
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy checks the first one to three armpit nodes.
- If they're clear, the other nodes can usually be left alone, which lowers the risk of arm swelling (lymphoedema).
- If cancer is found, more nodes may be removed.

### Breast reconstruction
- Can be done at the same time as mastectomy or later.
- Uses either implants or the patient's own tissue.
- Oncoplastic techniques can improve breast shape after lumpectomy.

### How to choose
The decision depends on tumour size, type and location; genetic status; breast size; and personal preference.

Questions to ask your surgeon:
1. Am I a candidate for breast-conserving surgery?
2. Would chemotherapy before surgery help?
3. Will I need radiation?
4. What are my reconstruction options?

### Recovery
- Most patients go home within one to two days after lumpectomy and a few days after mastectomy.
- Early arm exercises help keep shoulder movement.
- A surgical drain may stay in for several days.
- Support from family and other survivors is encouraged.

### Know the warning signs
Get these checked promptly: a painless lump, a change in breast shape, skin dimpling, nipple discharge, or a newly inverted nipple. People with a family history of breast or ovarian cancer should consider genetic counselling.

### Frequently asked questions
- **Is lumpectomy plus radiation as safe as mastectomy?** For suitable patients, yes. Outcomes are comparable.
- **Will I need chemotherapy?** It depends on several features of the tumour.
- **Can the cancer come back?** It can, which is why radiation, other treatments and regular follow-up matter.
